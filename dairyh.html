Dairy Hub

Designing a digital platform for dairy farmers

Introduction

Dairy Hub is a concept platform designed to support dairy farmers by improving access to farm inputs, equipment, and essential resources.

This project was driven by a deliberate decision to design from research, not assumption — including selecting a specific regional context to better understand real user needs and constraints.

The Problem

Small-scale dairy farmers often operate within fragmented systems where:

  • access to quality feed and equipment is inconsistent
  • suppliers are not centralized
  • information is scattered and unreliable

This creates inefficiencies in:

  • procurement
  • decision-making
  • farm productivity

More importantly, many existing digital solutions fail because they:

  • assume high digital literacy
  • prioritize features over usability
  • ignore the realities of rural and semi-urban users

Design Objective

To design a platform that:

  • simplifies access to farm inputs and equipment
  • supports informed decision-making
  • works within the constraints of real users
  • balances usability, accessibility, and scalability

Understanding the User

Rather than designing generically, I grounded this project in user profiling and contextual thinking.

Key considerations included:

  • varying levels of digital literacy
  • reliance on mobile devices
  • trust in digital transactions
  • need for clarity over complexity

Primary users:

  • small-scale dairy farmers
  • agricultural suppliers

My Role

UX Research User Profiling Wireframing UI Design (Web & Mobile) Brand Identity Design System Thinking

Process

1. Research & Framing

I began by identifying a specific problem space rather than designing a generic marketplace.

This allowed me to:

  • avoid assumption-driven design
  • ground decisions in context
  • define clearer user needs

2. Structuring the Experience

I mapped key user flows:

  • onboarding
  • browsing products
  • evaluating options
  • completing purchases

The goal was to:

reduce friction and cognitive load at every step

3. Wireframing

Wireframes focused on:

  • clarity over density
  • predictable navigation
  • logical grouping of information

At this stage, I prioritized:

  • usability
  • structure
  • flow

over visual detail.

4. Brand Identity

The visual system was intentionally designed to reflect:

  • Green → agriculture, growth, trust
  • Yellow → value, energy, accessibility

5. UI Design

I designed both:

  • mobile interfaces (primary use case)
  • supporting web presence

6. System Thinking

Rather than designing isolated screens, I built:

  • reusable components
  • scalable layouts
  • consistent interaction patterns

Key Design Decisions

Simplicity over complexity

Interfaces were intentionally kept minimal to reduce cognitive load.

Mobile-first approach

Users are more likely to access the platform via mobile devices.

Clear CTAs

Actions like purchasing were made obvious and accessible.

Visual consistency

A unified design system improves usability and trust.

Trade-offs & Considerations

  • Balancing simplicity with functionality
  • Designing for low digital literacy without oversimplifying
  • Ensuring scalability while maintaining clarity

Outcome

The result is a cohesive product ecosystem that:

  • improves access to farm resources
  • simplifies user interaction
  • demonstrates how digital tools can support agricultural communities

Reflection

This project reinforced my approach to design as:

a balance between user needs, technical feasibility, and business intent

It also strengthened my ability to:

  • design from research
  • think in systems
  • make intentional design decisions
